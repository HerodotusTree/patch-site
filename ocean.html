How to read these numbers

Every figure is verified. Counts come from script extraction over the filed chunk files, meter readings from live checks, times from the launch timestamp and the files' own timestamps. Nothing is estimated; where a number is approximate, it wears its ≈.

Failures are found on disk, not in reports. A leg that dies silently leaves no file, and the missing file is the report. Three legs died on rate limits today; all three were caught that way, re-filed, and their cost stayed inside the published times.

Staging is the climb; launch is release. The slow work — fetching, verifying, preparing — happens before the launch, where time is cheap. What launches is already finished growing; the burn is only the unfolding.

The meter is a floor, not a cap. It reads in whole percents while the work now moves in fractions of fractions beneath it. The watch stays on as an instrument. In testing mode, it constrains nothing.
