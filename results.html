Distributed Systems & Parallel Computing

Fan-out / fan-in at width 296. A three-layer architecture — orchestrator, ten parallel seed supervisors, leaf workers that cannot spawn — processing ~40,000 records per hour in measured burn windows. Rate-limit engineering: when the platform's limiter killed 15% of workers inside an ignition burst, the countermeasures were mechanical — staggered ignition, trickle gates, smaller blocks — and the kill rate went 15% → 1.4% → 0. Bottleneck re-architecture: serial ignition was measured as the true bottleneck (~2.1 s per worker through a single dispatcher); parallelizing the dispatchers cut a 296-worker startup from 53 minutes to ~215 seconds.

AI Orchestration & Context Engineering

Stateless workers. A width test exposed per-chunk cost creeping from ~1.4M to ~2.35M proxy tokens as workers inherited conversational context; the fix — every layer below the orchestrator carries payloads and tallies, never history — shaped all later design. Deterministic guardrails: classification is computed at staging by a small deterministic program (168 lines), validated value-by-value against already-filed work before it was trusted; workers write from its output and have never overridden it. Cost metering: throughput managed against a strict weekly quota with a measured conversion (~74 chunks per meter-percent, confirmed across three runs). No dollar figure is published — the platform exposes no dollar metering, and this page prints no estimates.

Data Engineering & Automated Auditing

Partitioning & recovery: 272,221 rows sliced into 25-record chunks with seam verification at every boundary, so any failed worker is re-dispatched against the same payload with zero data loss — 31 deaths, 31 recoveries, 0 chunks lost. Five independent checks stand between the catalogue and every published figure: per-chunk seam checks, whole-wave anchor and chain checks before launch, a parity gate on the arithmetic, an extraction audit after every run that re-reads the filings themselves (never worker summaries), and record verification against the census before any record is announced. Audit result to date: zero anomalies across 3,307 filings.

Observational Astronomy & Astrometry

A census defined on Gaia DR3: parallax between 6.0 and 6.525 mas, parallax_over_error > 10, ordered nearest-first — a complete spherical shell where no published census exists. Per system: parallax distance with error span, proper motion and tangential velocity, radial velocity and space motion where present, BP−RP photometry, astrometric quality (RUWE), multiplicity testimony, white-dwarf and disturbed-fit detection, and a Stefan–Boltzmann habitable-zone band, labeled derived wherever it is derived and filed as not-derivable wherever the data does not support it.
